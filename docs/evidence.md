# Evidence and limitations

This protocol combines plausible behavioral strategies, but the complete 15-minute plus 15-minute routine has not been clinically validated as a single intervention.

## What research supports

Digital switching can increase boredom and reduce attention, satisfaction, and perceived meaning during media consumption. This supports reducing rapid switching, but it does not prove that this specific protocol reverses those effects.

Tam KY, Inzlicht M. *Fast-forward to boredom: How switching behavior on digital media makes people more bored.* Journal of Experimental Psychology: General, 2024.  
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39158465/

A randomized trial of an app-based interruption reduced time spent on the most problematic app, while effects on problematic social-media use and self-efficacy were not significant. This supports friction as a potentially useful tool, not as a guaranteed solution.

*Promoting Self-Regulated Social Media Use on Smartphones With a Mobile Intervention App: Randomized Controlled Trial.*  
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13062480/

A one-week social-media break improved self-reported well-being, depression, and anxiety in one randomized trial. Another randomized reduction trial found no clear improvement in attention or well-being. Results therefore remain mixed and depend on the intervention and population.

https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35512731/  
https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34938826/

## What is not established

1. “Dopamine detox” or “dopamine reset” is not a literal description of the brain mechanism.
2. The 15-minute duration has not been established as uniquely effective.
3. Paper reading has not been shown here to biologically reset attention.
4. Grayscale may reduce visual appeal for some users, but it should not be presented as protecting eye health.
5. Personal improvement does not establish causation.

## Intended use

Use the protocol as a low-risk personal habit experiment. It is not a substitute for diagnosis or treatment. People experiencing severe distress, compulsive behavior, depression, anxiety, ADHD symptoms, or addiction should seek qualified professional support.
